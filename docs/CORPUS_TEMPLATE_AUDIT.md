# Corpus template audit: the whole Fractus CTE-Atom training corpus

Date: 2026-10-18, ~17:26-17:58 ET. Read-only analysis on the box. The pod trainer was not touched.

**Corpus.** `atom_corpus/atom_corpus.i16` (8,821,883,824 bytes = 4,410,941,912 ids) is the file the v3 trainer reads (`/workspace/atom_corpus.i16` on the pod, same size, same md5 for the first MB as the HF copy).

I verified byte for byte that it is the exact alphabetical concatenation of the 110 per-skill shards in `atom_corpus/*.i16` on HF (`scripts/corpus_audit/map.py`: every shard matches in full at its expected offset and the concatenation ends exactly at the file size).

All numbers below are therefore per training shard. Code: `scripts/corpus_audit/{map,audit,distinct,tf_shards}.py`. Raw data: `docs/corpus_audit_data/`.

## Method

- **Docs.** A doc is everything from one BOS (257) to the next. Every doc ends with `<B6><EOS>`.
- **Template share (16-gram, ≥5 other docs).** For each shard I sampled 2,000 docs (seed 0) and hashed every 16-gram of ids that does not cross a doc boundary. A position counts as *templated* if its 16-gram occurs in ≥6 distinct sampled docs, i.e. in ≥5 other docs. Because the sample is only 2,000 of the 7,500-11,400 docs, this is a lower bound.
- **Distinct 16-grams (full shard).** The exact number of distinct 16-gram types over all ids of each shard. I also computed it over the whole 4.41B-id corpus.
- **Prefix duplication.** For all docs: the share of docs whose first 64 ids after BOS equal those of another doc, and the number of distinct 64-id prefixes.
- **Cross-shard sharing.** 100 docs per shard: the share of a shard's 16-gram types that also occur in ≥2 other shards.

## Headline numbers

- **Weighted template share (16-grams in ≥5 other docs): 99.83%** of all training positions. Positions whose 16-gram occurs in at least one other sampled doc: 99.98%.
- **The whole 4,410,941,912-id corpus contains only 243,575 distinct 16-grams.** That is one distinct 16-gram per ~18,100 ids, and the corpus's entire novel content is on the order of a few hundred KB of text, recombined.
  - Per 41M-id shard: 19,060-21,196 distinct 16-grams (median 20,474).
  - The per-shard counts sum to 2,250,905, 9.2x the corpus-wide count, so most 16-gram types recur across many shards (shared scaffolding).
- **Prefix duplication is 100% in every shard.** Each shard has only 41-199 distinct 64-id openings (median 74) for 7,500-11,400 docs.
- **Docs are long and uniform.** The mean is 3,347-3,748 ids per doc. 1,215,000 docs in total, 11,400 per skill shard and 7,500 per applied_neuroscience shard.
- **Spread across shards is tiny.** Template share ranges from 99.81% to 99.94%.
- **No existing shard is real varied text.** All 110 are generated by the same slot-filling templates, for example:
  - "What is X? Walk through it as a <format> from a <angle> perspective, aimed at <audience>." followed by "Here is a treatment of **X** from a ... angle";
  - "**Core principles:** 1..5" with the principles shuffled;
  - "The example illustrates how the abstract principle becomes a concrete decision. The decision-maker must (1) recognize...".
  - The applied_neuroscience shards use a different template ("From a neuroscience perspective, **X** is ..."). That is why they share fewer 16-gram types with other shards (52-56% vs 87-92%) while still being the most templated internally.

## Recommendation

**What the existing shards are.** All 110 shards (100% of the 4.41B ids) are template text. None is real varied text. Their combined unique content (~244k distinct 16-grams) would fit in well under 1% of one shard.

Keeping a small slice is fine for the skill formats, for example 5-10% of the new mix, taken from the less rigid skill shards. Repeating them is pointless: 4.41B ids is the same few hundred KB of text seen ~18,000 times.

**Budget.** About $19 of credit buys ~37 h of one 5090 at the measured 10.6k ids/s, i.e. **~1.4B ids**. The Atomizer gives ~1.04 ids per UTF-8 byte (measured on English prose and on the corpus), so the budget is **~1.35 GB of raw text for one pass**.
